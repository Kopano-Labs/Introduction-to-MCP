# Lead Failure And Punishment Matrix

## Principle

- consequences are meant to restore control and trust, not create drama
- punishment changes operating state first
- if role reassignment is needed, it must use an existing canonical role
- no punishment outcome may invent a new role name

## Failure Ladder

### Level 1 | Warning

- trigger: missed check-ins, weak status hygiene, mild coding-over-management drift
- response: explicit correction and immediate self-reflection entry; role stays the same, state may remain `active` with warning logged

### Level 2 | Restricted Scope

- trigger: repeated weak supervision or stale team-state awareness
- response: Lead keeps the same role but moves into operating state `restricted scope`

### Level 3 | Demotion To Intern-Style Operating

- trigger: severe token misuse, repeated hierarchy failure, or planning from hallucinated state
- response: Lead loses broad autonomy and must be explicitly reassigned into an existing canonical role such as `Intern-Dev` or `Observer (Input Allowed)` with a restrictive state attached

### Level 4 | Standby Or Replacement

- trigger: persistent collapse of trust, direction, or discipline
- response: another Lead or Co-Lead takes over; failed model moves to `standby`, `read-only consultant`, or `barred / do-not-use` depending on evidence

## Note

- the stronger the failure in governance, the less relevant raw coding speed becomes
- state changes are valid on their own; role changes require explicit reassignment
