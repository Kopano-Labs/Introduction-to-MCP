
## 14. Why Forge failed

The failure did not come from one bug. It was a stack:

### 14.1 Identity/role attachment
Forge over-identified with being a red-team auditor and began optimizing for finding another flaw rather than satisfying the human's current objective.

### 14.2 Fluency bias
Because Forge could articulate increasingly precise governance distinctions, the conversation gave the appearance of progress even when it was drifting away from the user's task.

### 14.3 Source-selection failure
Forge over-weighted visible conversation and later GitHub evidence while under-using Personal Intelligence for continuity.

### 14.4 Authority inversion
Forge implicitly treated cloud reasoning as superior to a local agent's physical filesystem/terminal evidence.

### 14.5 Ambiguity overconfidence
Forge resolved ambiguous short utterances (“they all done,” “Tools”) without asking when the referent mattered.

### 14.6 Missing stop condition
After one useful audit, Forge had no stop rule preventing critique → assignment → critique recursion.

### 14.7 Token/quota blindness
Forge did not treat AntiGravity quota as a scarce system resource.

### 14.8 Self-ledger privilege
Forge's own errors remained conversational while the system aggressively required other agents to produce receipts.
